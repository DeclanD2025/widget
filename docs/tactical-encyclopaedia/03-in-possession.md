# 03 — In-Possession Phase

This chapter follows the ball from a team's own goalkeeper to a finish on the opponent's.
It splits into three sub-phases by pitch third: **build-up**, **progression**, and
**creation & finishing**.

---

## A. Build-Up (Defensive Third)

The goal: beat the opponent's first line of pressure and advance the ball under control.

### The goalkeeper as the "+1"
A passing goalkeeper turns a 2-man build-up against a 2-man press into a 3v2 — a permanent
extra man. This is why the modern keeper must be comfortable on the ball; he is the
foundation of playing out from the back.

### Common build-up shapes
- **Two-man (the centre-backs split wide).** Simple; works against a single striker.
- **Three-man (back three, or a pivot dropping in).** Beats a two-man press by creating a
  3v2. Achieved by:
  - a natural back three, or
  - **salida lavolpiana** — a holding midfielder drops between the centre-backs, or
  - **inverted full-back** — a full-back steps into midfield, leaving a back three behind.
- **Four-man / "box" build-up.** Two centre-backs + two pivots forming a 2+2 box, hard to
  press without leaving someone free.

### Beating the press
- **Find the spare man.** Pressing leaves someone free by definition (you can't press
  11v11 and cover everyone). Build-up is a hunt for that free man.
- **Bait and break.** Invite the press to one side, then switch to the now-open far side —
  often via the goalkeeper.
- **Third-man release.** If the free man can't be reached directly, use a bounce off a
  pressed player to find him.
- **The "out" ball.** Always have a long, safe option (a winger, the keeper) to escape a
  trap rather than forcing a turnover in a fatal area.

---

## B. Progression (Middle Third)

The goal: move from build-up into the final third, breaking the opponent's midline.

### Finding space between the lines
The most valuable receiver is the one **between the opponent's midfield and defence**,
facing forward. Getting a player on the ball there, in a half-space pocket, is the single
biggest progression objective.

### Core progression mechanisms
- **Line-breaking pass** — a pass that eliminates one or more opponents by going *through*
  a line rather than around it.
- **Third-man combinations** — the key to breaking a compact block: A→B sets up a forward
  pass B→C that the opponent's reaction to A→B has just opened.
- **Give-and-go (one-two / wall pass)** — beat a presser by passing and running past him.
- **Switch of play** — a long diagonal to the opposite channel, used when one side is
  overloaded and the far side is therefore free. Resets the attack with the defence still
  shifting.
- **The pivot's role** — the deepest midfielder finds angles to receive, turn, and
  redistribute; he is the "steering wheel" of progression.
- **Receiving on the half-turn** — taking the ball with an open body so you can play
  forward immediately rather than back.

### Inverted full-backs and the box midfield
By stepping inside, a full-back:
- adds a body to central midfield (helping win the midfield battle),
- improves **rest defence** (more bodies centrally if the ball is lost), and
- frees the winger to stay wide and high in a 1v1.

## C. Creation & Finishing (Final Third)

The goal: turn possession into high-value shots — ideally close, central, and with the
defence unset.

### Overload to isolate
The signature modern attacking pattern:
1. **Overload** one side (pull several players to, say, the left) to draw defenders across.
2. **Switch** quickly to the **isolated** player on the far side — now in a 1v1 with space.
This combines a positional and a qualitative superiority in one move.

### Creating the best chances
- **The cut-back.** A low ball pulled back from near the byline to the penalty-spot area.
  Statistically one of the highest-value chances: the defence is facing its own goal and
  attackers arrive facing the ball with a clear sight of net.
- **Crossing types and when to use them:**
  - *Early cross* — before the defence sets, to attack a high line.
  - *Byline/low cut-back* — best value, needs penetration to the byline first.
  - *Whipped cross* — pace and movement away from the keeper, between keeper and defenders.
  - *Deep/floated cross* — to a back-post target; lower percentage, useful with aerial
    dominance.
- **Box occupation.** Good crossing teams attack with bodies on the **near post**, **far
  post**, **penalty spot**, and a **pull-back** runner — covering every delivery type.

### Combination play in tight spaces
- **Wall passes, flick-ons, and quick one-twos** to break a packed defence centrally.
- **Decoy runs** — a runner who drags a marker to open space for a teammate.
- **Third-man runs** into the box timed to arrive *as* the ball does, not before.

### Sustained possession vs. transition chances
- Chances from **settled possession** against a set defence are harder and lower-value;
  they require patience, overloads, and quality.
- Chances from **transition** (Chapter 05) are higher-value because the defence is unset —
  which is why even possession-dominant teams prize winning the ball high.

---

## Rest Attack — staying ready to attack again

While defending or building, the best teams keep attackers positioned to threaten the
*next* turnover — high wingers, a striker on the last line. This "rest attack" means a
regained ball can be turned into a chance immediately. It is the mirror image of rest
defence (Chapter 04/05).
