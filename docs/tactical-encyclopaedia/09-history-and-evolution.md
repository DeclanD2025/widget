# 09 — History & Evolution

Tactics evolve as a conversation: every dominant idea provokes a counter, which provokes a
counter to the counter. Knowing the lineage helps you understand *why* a modern team does
what it does — and predict how opponents will try to stop it.

---

## The evolution of shape

| Era | Dominant shape | The idea |
|-----|----------------|----------|
| 1880s–1920s | **2-3-5 ("the Pyramid")** | Attack-heavy; five forwards, minimal defence |
| 1920s–1930s | **W-M (3-2-2-3)** | Response to the 1925 offside-law change; pulled a midfielder back to defend |
| 1950s–60s | **4-2-4 / 4-3-3** | Brazil's World Cup sides; a true back four and midfield balance |
| 1960s | **Catenaccio** | Italian sweeper system; defence as a coached art |
| 1970s | **Total Football** | Dutch universal interchange and high pressing |
| 1980s–90s | **4-4-2** | Compact two banks of four; pressing zones (Sacchi's Milan) |
| 2000s–10s | **4-2-3-1 / 4-3-3** | Tactical flexibility; the rise of the holding midfielder |
| 2010s–now | **Fluid / phase-dependent shapes** | Teams change formation by phase (e.g. 4-3-3 → 3-2-5) |

The trend is clear: from **fixed positions** toward **fluid, phase-specific structures**,
and from defending or attacking as the priority toward **controlling all four phases**.

---

## The schools of thought

Different football cultures developed distinct philosophies that still shape the game:

- **Dutch / Total Football** — space, interchange, pressing, the high line. The root of
  modern positional play.
- **Italian defensive tradition** — organisation, the libero, the coached low block; the
  ancestor of modern defensive structure and game management.
- **South American (Argentine & Brazilian)** — flair, the enganche/No. 10, and more
  recently *relationism*; combinations and individual creativity.
- **English direct** — pace, power, crossing, second balls, set-piece strength.
- **Spanish positional** — possession with purpose, the rondo culture, La Masia.
- **German pressing (Rangnick school)** — gegenpressing, vertical transitions, intensity.

## The minds (a tactical family tree)

- **Rinus Michels / Johan Cruyff** — Total Football → the Barcelona philosophy.
- **Arrigo Sacchi** — zonal defending, pressing, and compactness with Milan; defending as a
  collective system, not individual duels.
- **Marcelo Bielsa** — extreme man-oriented pressing and verticality; a huge influence on
  Guardiola, Pochettino and others ("Bielsa-ism").
- **Pep Guardiola** — the modern apex of positional play; inverted full-backs, the box
  midfield, positional discipline.
- **Jürgen Klopp / Ralf Rangnick** — gegenpressing and transition football.
- **José Mourinho** — pragmatic, transition-and-block mastery; managing games and space.
- **Carlo Ancelotti** — adaptive, player-led balance; winning without a rigid dogma.
- **Roberto De Zerbi** — provocative build-up; baiting the press to break it.

You can read most modern teams as a blend of these influences: e.g. "a Bielsa-style press
bolted onto a Guardiola-style build-up".

---

## How rule changes reshaped tactics

Tactics don't only evolve from ideas — the laws of the game force change:

- **1925 offside law** (reduced from three defenders to two) → birth of the W-M.
- **1992 back-pass law** (keeper can't handle a deliberate back-pass) → keepers became
  footballers; the foundation of modern build-up and the sweeper-keeper.
- **Offside interpretation & VAR** → finer margins on the high line and the offside trap.
- **Goal-kick law change (2019)** (ball in play inside the box) → easier short build-up,
  encouraging playing out from the back.

## The data revolution

Analytics gave tactics a measurement language and changed decisions:

- **xG (expected goals)** — values *chance quality*, redirecting teams toward high-value
  shots (central, close, cut-backs) over speculative ones.
- **xT (expected threat)** — values every action by how much it increases the chance of
  scoring soon, valuing progression and zone entries, not just shots and assists.
- **PPDA** — measures pressing intensity, making "how aggressively do they press?"
  quantifiable.
- **Packing** — counts opponents bypassed by a pass or carry, valuing line-breaking.
- **Possession value / on-ball models** — value each touch's contribution, informing
  recruitment and in-game decisions.

The practical effect: clubs now *design* for set pieces, high turnovers, and high-value
chance types — because the data shows where goals actually come from. This is also why a
modern match-reading method (Chapter 10) blends the eye with these concepts.
