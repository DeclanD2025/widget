# 01 — Formations

A formation is a **starting reference**, not a team's identity. The same 4-3-3 can press
high or sit deep; modern teams change shape between phases (e.g. defend in a 4-4-2, attack
in a 3-2-5). Read formations as *where players begin* and *what relationships they create*,
not as a fixed map of the 90 minutes.

Each entry uses a fixed template: **Structure · Strengths · Weaknesses · Ideal personnel ·
How to beat it.**

---

## How to read a formation notation

Numbers run from defence to attack, **excluding the goalkeeper**:
`4-3-3` = 4 defenders, 3 midfielders, 3 forwards.
A four-number shape (`4-2-3-1`) splits the midfield into lines. The same eleven can be
described different ways depending on which phase you freeze.

## Back-Four Families

### 4-4-2 (flat)
- **Structure:** two banks of four, two strikers. The classic.
- **Strengths:** simple, compact, two solid defensive lines; natural width; two strikers
  to press and to attack crosses.
- **Weaknesses:** can be outnumbered in central midfield (2v3); the gap between the two
  banks can be exploited by a No. 10.
- **Ideal personnel:** hard-working wide midfielders, a strike partnership (one runner,
  one link), disciplined central pair.
- **How to beat it:** overload central midfield 3v2; play between the lines; pin the full-backs
  with wingers and attack the channels outside the midfield four.

### 4-4-2 Diamond
- **Structure:** defensive midfield anchor, two shuttlers, a No. 10, two strikers. No
  natural wide midfielders.
- **Strengths:** controls central midfield 4-deep; great for central combinations and
  pressing the centre.
- **Weaknesses:** **no natural width** — full-backs must provide it, leaving space in
  behind. Vulnerable to quick switches to the flanks.
- **How to beat it:** switch play to isolate the full-backs; use wide overloads; attack the
  space behind the advancing full-backs.

### 4-2-3-1
- **Structure:** double pivot, three attacking midfielders (two wide, one central No. 10),
  a lone striker. The modern default for two decades.
- **Strengths:** balance — the double pivot protects the defence and frees the No. 10;
  flexible between attack and defence; easy to morph into 4-3-3 or 4-5-1.
- **Weaknesses:** the lone striker can be isolated; relies heavily on the No. 10's form.
- **How to beat it:** press the double pivot to cut supply; deny the No. 10 the pockets;
  pin both full-backs so the wide players can't both attack.

### 4-3-3
- **Structure:** a midfield trio (one pivot + two No. 8s, or two pivots + a 10), and a
  front three of a striker and two wingers.
- **Strengths:** dominates midfield with a 3; superb for positional play and high pressing;
  wingers stretch the pitch and isolate full-backs.
- **Weaknesses:** demands very fit, intelligent midfielders; if a No. 8 is caught upfield,
  the pivot is exposed.
- **How to beat it:** target the space either side of the single pivot; counter quickly into
  the gaps left by attacking full-backs and high wingers.

### 4-1-4-1
- **Structure:** a lone holding midfielder, a bank of four ahead, a lone striker.
- **Strengths:** an excellent defensive mid-block; two compact lines of four-plus-one;
  good for controlling a game without the ball.
- **Weaknesses:** can be passive; the lone pivot covers huge ground; striker isolated.
- **How to beat it:** pull the pivot out of position and attack the vacated centre; quick
  combinations through the half-spaces.

## Back-Three / Back-Five Families

A back three becomes a back five when the wing-backs drop, and a 3-2 build-up when the
full-backs of a back four invert — the line is fluid.

### 3-5-2
- **Structure:** three centre-backs, two wing-backs, a midfield three, two strikers.
- **Strengths:** central solidity (3 CBs + 3 mids), two strikers, wing-backs give width;
  great for overloading midfield and defending the centre.
- **Weaknesses:** if a wing-back is beaten or pinned, the wide centre-back is dragged out;
  demands extraordinarily fit wing-backs.
- **How to beat it:** attack the wing-backs and the space behind them; overload one flank
  to pin the wing-back and isolate the wide CB.

### 3-4-3
- **Structure:** back three, two central mids, two wing-backs, a front three.
- **Strengths:** very aggressive — five attackers when wing-backs push; strong high press;
  occupies all five channels in attack.
- **Weaknesses:** only two central midfielders can be overrun; huge space to defend if
  transitions are lost.
- **How to beat it:** overload the two-man midfield; counter fast into the space behind the
  wing-backs.

### 3-4-2-1 (3-4-3 "box" / with two 10s)
- **Structure:** back three, double pivot, two free attacking midfielders behind a lone
  striker.
- **Strengths:** the two "10s" occupy the half-spaces — football's most valuable zones;
  superb central penetration and counter-pressing.
- **Weaknesses:** width comes only from wing-backs; the lone striker needs support quickly.
- **How to beat it:** deny the half-space pockets; force play wide and defend the crosses.

### 5-3-2 / 5-4-1 (defensive)
- **Structure:** the back-three shapes with wing-backs dropped into a five — a deliberate
  low block.
- **Strengths:** extremely hard to break down centrally; five across the back denies the
  cut-back zone; ideal for protecting a lead or as an underdog.
- **Weaknesses:** invites pressure; concedes territory; needs a real outlet to relieve.
- **How to beat it:** patient possession to drag the block; quick switches; crosses and
  set pieces; shots from distance to make them step out.

## Phase-Dependent & Hybrid Shapes

Modern elite teams rarely hold one shape. Common transformations:

- **4-3-3 → 3-2-5 in possession:** a full-back inverts into midfield, the other pushes high,
  forming a back three, a double pivot, and five attackers across the front line.
- **4-2-3-1 → 2-3-5 / "W-M" build-up:** full-backs advance, pivots split, five forward.
- **4-4-2 → 4-2-4 / 4-1-3-2:** wide midfielders push on to press or attack.
- **Back four ↔ back three:** a holding midfielder drops between the centre-backs
  (*salida lavolpiana*) to make a three and beat a two-man press.

> **Watch for the build-up shape, not the team-sheet shape.** When a team has the ball in
> its own third, count the players in each line — that tells you its real plan.

---

## The Myth of the Formation

Two teams in "4-3-3" can play opposite football. Formation tells you *starting positions
and relationships*; **style** (Chapter 02), **roles** (Chapter 07), and **phase behaviour**
tell you the actual game plan. Always pair "what shape?" with "what are they trying to do
with it?"
