# 14 — Opposition Analysis & Game-Planning

This chapter is the **analyst's workflow**: how a coaching staff studies an opponent and
builds a game plan. It is the professional version of the pre-match reading in Chapter 10,
and it doubles as a template for *your own* scouting notes.

---

## 1. The scouting workflow

A typical opposition report is built in stages:

1. **Watch 3–5 recent matches** — ideally including one vs. a similar style to your own.
2. **Establish their identity by phase** (the four phases of Ch. 03–05):
   - build-up shape and routes,
   - how they progress and where they create,
   - defensive block height and pressing scheme,
   - transition behaviour (counter vs. counterpress).
3. **Map personnel** — key individuals, roles, strong/weak foot, who the team plays through.
4. **Find the patterns** (Ch. 11) — their go-to automatisms in each phase.
5. **Find the weaknesses** — the seam your team can attack.
6. **Convert to a plan** — *with* the ball, *without* the ball, and in *transition*; plus
   set pieces and individual instructions.

---

## 2. What to log in each phase

### Their build-up (you defend)
- Shape (2, 3, or 4 at the back) and who drops/inverts.
- Goalkeeper: does he play short, and how good is he under pressure?
- Their *escape* route when pressed (a target man? a favourite switch?).
- **Your plan:** where to set the press trap; which pass to concede; who picks up the +1.

### Their attack (you defend)
- Which channel they favour (a dangerous winger? overloaded half-space?).
- Their chance-creation pattern (cut-backs? crosses? through-balls?).
- Set-piece routines and their best aerial threats.
- **Your plan:** block their primary pattern; protect the zone they target; mark their
  outlet.

### Your attack (they defend)
- Their block height and pressing triggers (Ch. 12).
- The seam: a slow centre-back? an attacking full-back who leaves space? a two-man midfield
  to overload?
- **Your plan:** the pattern (Ch. 11) that attacks that seam; where to create your overload.

### Transitions (both ways)
- Do they counterpress or drop? How quick is their counter, and from where?
- Their rest-defence numbers when they attack (how exposed on the counter?).
- **Your plan:** your rest defence to survive their counter; your outlet to exploit theirs.

## 3. The "how to hurt them / how they hurt us" sheet

Distil the whole report into two columns — this is what players actually need:

```
HOW WE HURT THEM                  | HOW THEY HURT US
----------------------------------|----------------------------------
- Overload their weak-side FB     | - Their RW 1v1 vs our LB
- Their CBs slow on the turn:     | - Counter through the channels when
  balls in behind                 |   our FBs are high
- Their 2-man midfield: play      | - Inswinging corners to their big CB
  through a 3                     |
- Set pieces: target their zonal  | - Quick switch after we overload
  far post                        |
```

---

## 4. Game-planning: three plans, not one

Good staffs prepare for the match *as it changes*:

- **Plan A — the intended approach** when the game is even.
- **Plan B — when chasing** (behind, or need a goal): a higher line, an extra attacker,
  more risk in build-up, target the patterns that beat their block.
- **Plan C — when protecting** (ahead, or under pressure): drop the block, secure rest
  defence, manage the game (Ch. 17).

Each plan names the *triggers* to switch (e.g. "if 0-1 down after 60', go to Plan B:
on for a midfielder, to a back three in possession").

---

## 5. Individual instructions

The plan ends with **player-specific** jobs, e.g.:
- "No. 6 — screen the No. 10; don't get dragged wide."
- "Left-back — stay home vs. their right winger; don't overlap until we're settled."
- "Striker — press the right centre-back on the curve, show him inside."
- "Set pieces — you pick up their No. 4 man-to-man."

Tactics only work when they are translated into clear individual responsibilities.
