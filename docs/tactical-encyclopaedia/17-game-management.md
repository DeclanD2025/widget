# 17 — Game Management & In-Game Adjustments

Tactics are not set at kick-off and left alone. This chapter covers how teams *manage* a
match as it unfolds — by score, by time, by momentum — and how to read those adjustments.
It is the dynamic counterpart to the static plans of Chapters 01–02.

---

## 1. Game states (the score changes the game)

The score line silently rewrites both teams' tactics:

| Score state | The leading team tends to… | The trailing team tends to… |
|-------------|----------------------------|------------------------------|
| **0-0 early** | stick to plan, probe, stay balanced | same — feel each other out |
| **1-0 / ahead** | drop the block, secure rest defence, slow tempo, manage time | push up, commit numbers, take more risk |
| **Behind** | (see "trailing") | higher line, extra attacker, more direct, chase the game |
| **Comfortably ahead** | game-manage, rotate, kill tempo | often break shape and concede more |

> **Reading tip:** the moment a goal goes in, *predict the shift before it happens* — the
> leader will sit, the chaser will commit, and **space opens in midfield** for the trailing
> team (and in behind for the leader's counter).

---

## 2. The levers a manager can pull

In-game adjustments come from a finite toolbox:

- **Change the block height** — press higher to chase, drop deeper to protect (Ch. 12).
- **Change the shape** — e.g. back four → back three to add a centre-back when protecting, or
  a midfielder → striker to chase.
- **Change personnel** — fresh legs (especially for a fading press), a different profile (a
  target man to go direct; a dribbler to beat a low block).
- **Change the target** — instruct attacks down a flank where a mismatch has appeared.
- **Change the tempo** — speed up (more vertical, more risk) or slow down (control, kill the
  game).

### Reading substitutions
- **Like-for-like** → stabilise / freshen, no change of plan.
- **Shape-changing sub** → the manager is committing to chase or protect; expect the game to
  open or close.
- **Attacker for defender** (or vice-versa) → a clear statement of intent; re-score your
  Battle Map for the new shape.

---

## 3. Momentum — real, but readable

Momentum isn't mystical; it has tactical *causes* you can name:

- **Territorial creep** — one team's line of engagement keeps advancing; the other is pinned.
- **Repeated same-side success** — a flank mismatch being exploited again and again.
- **A tiring press** — the chasing team can't get out; turnovers pile up.
- **A goal's psychological swing** — combined with the game-state shift above.

When you sense momentum, **find its tactical cause** — that tells you whether it will last
(a structural mismatch) or pass (a hot five minutes).

---

## 4. Time and the clock

Time is a tactical resource:

- **Game-killing (protecting a lead):** slow restarts, keep the ball in the corners, make
  substitutions to break rhythm, force the play wide and deep where it's safe.
- **Time-recovery (chasing):** quick restarts, go long earlier, throw centre-backs forward
  for set pieces and late crosses, take more 1v1 risks.
- **The final 15 minutes** are their own phase: fatigue stretches blocks (Ch. 04), subs
  change match-ups, and game state dominates — a disproportionate share of goals comes here.

---

## 5. Fatigue and its tactical signature

As legs tire (typically 60–75' onward):

- **Compactness drops** — the gap between defence and midfield grows; the "between the lines"
  pocket opens (Ch. 03/04).
- **Pressing intensity fades** — triggers are missed; build-up teams get more time.
- **Transition defence weakens** — recovery runs slow; counters become more dangerous.
- **Substitutions reset** local match-ups — a fresh winger vs. a tired full-back is a classic
  late-game lever.

> **Predictive use:** if a team relies on an intense high press, weight its threat *early*
> and watch for the block to crack late — that's often when the game's decisive goals land.

---

## 6. Reading adjustments live — a checklist

1. **On every goal:** name the game-state shift and where space will now appear.
2. **On every sub:** classify it (like-for-like / shape / intent) and re-score the match-up.
3. **Track the lines of engagement:** are they creeping up or being pushed back? (momentum)
4. **Watch the 60–75' window:** where is the block cracking as legs go?
5. **Late game:** who is managing the clock and who is chasing? Tactics now bend almost
   entirely to game state.

---

## 7. How this feeds prediction

Game management is why a kick-off prediction must be a *living model* (Ch. 10/16):

- A "Likely A" can become "B back in it" the instant A leads and invites pressure poorly.
- The **upset route** you wrote on the worksheet usually arrives *through* a game-state
  shift — name it in advance and you'll see it coming.

> The best readers of football don't just predict the *result*; they predict the *next
> twenty minutes* — the shift that the score, the clock, and the tiring legs are about to
> force.
